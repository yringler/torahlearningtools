# Where the sources differ (Steinsaltz vs. Bartenura)

These are the places worth a parent's look. They're collected from the agents that tied each piece's meaning to
Steinsaltz (primary) and Bartenura (tr. R. Shraga Silverstein). The **Steinsaltz** view is always a verbatim quote.
These notes are about the **Simple** lines.

## Perek 3–4
- **3:2:** Steinsaltz says start Shema only if you can finish the whole thing before reaching the mourners' rows. Bartenura says finishing one section is enough. The Simple line follows Steinsaltz.
- **3:4–3:6:** Bartenura notes that Ezra's rule requiring a mikvah before Torah and davening was later cancelled. Steinsaltz doesn't mention this. The Simple lines keep these topics vague on purpose.
- **4:1:** Steinsaltz says Maariv has no fixed time and can be said all night. Bartenura reads the wording as showing Maariv was once optional (today it is required).
- **4:4:** For פָּרָשַׁת הָעִבּוּר, Steinsaltz leaves the meaning to the Gemara, and the Simple line says "at every time of change". Bartenura says "even when they stray into sin" (shown as an explanation). The Simple line's "makes his davening a chore" follows Bartenura; Steinsaltz just says "fixed".
- **4:5:** Steinsaltz says the rider gets off his donkey to daven. Bartenura says the halacha doesn't follow this, and he does not get off.
